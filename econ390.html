About the course

This course provides an introduction to labor economics, with a focus on real-world application and policy issues. Labor economics is—broadly—the study of the exchange of labor for wages. The purpose of this course is not to provide “yes” or “no” answers, but to teach you how to engage with economic models and empirical evidence, and reach informed opinions about the numerous policy questions regarding labor markets in the United States and other countries.

  1. Part 1FundamentalsLabor supply, labor demand, and equilibrium; compensating differentials, incentive pay, unemployment and recessions.
  2. Part 2Mobility and OpportunityEducation, training, and human capital; inequality, intergenerational mobility, and anti-poverty programs.
  3. Part 3Imperfect MarketsMonopsony, the minimum wage, unions and collective bargaining, discrimination, and negotiation.
  4. Part 4Labor & SpaceLabor mobility, commuting, and immigration.
