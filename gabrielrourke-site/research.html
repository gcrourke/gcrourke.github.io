Hospital Consolidation and the Labor Market for Clinical Staff

Abstract

Between 2016 and 2022, hospital mergers and acquisitions increased concentration in many local health care markets. This paper asks whether that consolidation changed employment and wages for registered nurses, nurse practitioners, and physician assistants. I link Medicare ownership-change and provider-enrollment records (CMS CHOW and PECOS) to occupational employment and wage data from the BLS (OEWS) across U.S. metropolitan areas, and estimate effects with a staggered difference-in-differences design and event studies.

Resort Town Shortages: Effect of J-1 Visa Restrictions on the Labor Market Outcomes of Natives

Abstract

In June 2020, a presidential proclamation suspended entry for participants in the J-1 Summer Work Travel program and several other groups of temporary foreign workers. Participation in the program fell from 108,301 in 2019 to 4,952 in 2020. I use this shock and a synthetic control design to estimate its effects on employment, average weekly wages, and unemployment among native workers.

Reserve Requirements in the 21st Century: Understanding the Monetary Policy Tools of Developing Economies

with Rodrigo Razo Solares

Abstract

Reserve requirements oblige banks to hold a minimum share of their deposits as liquid assets. First used in the 1800s to build trust in banks and manage liquidity, they later became a tool for controlling the money supply. Many central banks have since abandoned them, while others, including Brazil, China, and India, adjust them frequently as part of monetary policy. We argue that reserve requirements are an effective tool for countries trying to limit large capital inflows, either by withdrawing domestic liquidity or by acting countercyclically to discourage inflows.
