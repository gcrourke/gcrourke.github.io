Hospital Consolidation and the Labor Market for Clinical Staff

Between 2016 and 2022, hospital mergers and acquisitions increased concentration in many local health care markets. This paper asks whether that consolidation changed employment and wages for registered nurses, nurse practitioners, and physician assistants. I link Medicare ownership-change and provider-enrollment records (CMS CHOW and PECOS) to occupational employment and wage data from the BLS (OEWS) across U.S. metropolitan areas, and estimate effects with a staggered difference-in-differences design and event studies.

Resort Town Shortages: Effect of J-1 Visa Restrictions on the Labor Market Outcomes of Natives

In the summer of 2020, numerous changes were made to immigration policy in response to the Covid-19 pandemic. One such change was Proclamation 10052, which extended Proclamation 10014 and suspended entry for numerous visa holders. One particularly affected group was the J-1 Summer Work Travel (SWT) program, where participation went from 108,301 participants in 2019 to 4,952 in 2020. This provides a unique opportunity to estimate the labor market impact of immigration. Using a synthetic control method, I estimate the effects of this sudden immigration shock on employment levels, average weekly wages, and unemployment.

Reserve Requirements in the 21st Century: Understanding the Monetary Policy Tools of Developing Economies (with Rodrigo Razo Solares)

Reserve requirements are a form of banking regulation that requires depository institutions to hold a minimum amount of liquid assets relative to their deposit liabilities, a proportion called the reserve ratio. Starting in the 1800s, they were used to promote trust in banks and security in the liquidity market, and later as a form of monetary policy to control the money supply. In recent years they have fallen out of favor with central banks, many of which have scrapped them completely. Some countries keep low and stable requirements as a formality, while others (Brazil, China, India) change them often and use them as a monetary policy tool. We find that reserve requirements are an effective monetary policy tool for countries trying to limit the effect of high capital inflows, either by withdrawing domestic liquidity or as a countercyclical action that disincentivizes capital inflows.
